Note from the author

What It’s Always Been About

I have always believed that every problem has a solution. Growing up in a first-generation family taught me that challenges are opportunities to learn, adapt, and build something better. I carried this philosophy into SUNY Downstate’s ABSN Program, determined not to leave my technology background behind, but to discover how it could contribute to the greater whole.

I wanted to continue the pioneering spirit of technology’s early innovators, who challenged us to think different while keeping the humanity in healthcare at the center of positive outcomes. Innovation in healthcare must enhance, not replace. I believe meaningful progress requires understanding both sides of healthcare technology: behind the screen and at the bedside. Only with both perspectives can we begin to create meaningful change.

I approached my clinical education as an opportunity to put that belief into practice. While navigating the demands of an accelerated program, I began identifying friction points in documentation, studying, and clinical preparation. Drawing on my software development experience, I created tools from scratch to simplify these processes without taking away from a nurse’s ability to think critically or disrupting established workflows.

What began as personal experiments evolved through testing, feedback, and collaboration with classmates. Sharing these tools taught me that innovation is not about having every answer, but about listening, refining, and recognizing what remains to be learned.

These projects represent my desire to build tools that will help future nurses. By starting this project on day one of my nursing journey at Downstate, I had an opportunity to create projects that support the mission and vision of the College of Nursing, enrich the student experience, and make things easier for my friends and classmates.

Downstate gave me an opportunity to explore this intersection. I hope to carry that spirit forward, demonstrating that nurses can help shape the technologies transforming their profession. Yet, I’m just simply thankful for the opportunity to help where I could.

Best,
George
